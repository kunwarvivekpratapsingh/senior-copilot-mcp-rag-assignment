# Lockout/Tagout for Pump Systems

## When this applies

Any intervention that requires opening the pressure boundary, removing a guard, or
working within the coupling envelope. This includes strainer cleaning on systems
without a standby strainer.

## Sequence

1. Obtain a permit. No exceptions for "quick" jobs - the majority of incidents on
   rotating equipment occur during work expected to take under fifteen minutes.
2. Stop the pump at the local control station and confirm zero speed visually.
3. Isolate electrically. Lock the breaker open and apply personal locks.
4. Isolate process. Close and lock suction and discharge valves.
5. Depressurise and drain through the designated vent. Confirm zero pressure at the
   local gauge, not from the control system.
6. Verify zero energy. Attempt a start from the local station.
7. Apply tags recording who, when, and why.

## Specific hazards

**Stored pressure.** A closed discharge valve can retain full system pressure after
the pump stops. Always vent before breaking a flange.

**Thermal.** Boiler feed pump casings remain above 100 degC for a considerable period
after shutdown. Allow to cool or use appropriate protection.

**Rotation from the driven end.** A pump in a common header can be driven backwards
by flow from a running parallel pump. Isolate the discharge before assuming zero
rotation.

## Removal

Locks are removed only by the person who applied them, after confirming personnel are
clear and guards are refitted.
